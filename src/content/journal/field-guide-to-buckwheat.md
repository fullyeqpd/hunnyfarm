Buckwheat honey is very different from most honey. It is dark, nearly black in the jar, rich, and much less sweet than clover or wildflower, with bold notes of molasses, toasted toffee and a deep earthy aroma. Many people compare it to dark molasses or malt rather than to honey at all.

That is why it is so popular with anyone who grew up on Eastern European-style honey, and why it can catch a first-timer off guard. If you have never tried it, buy the one-pound jar before you buy more.

## Where it shines

On dark rye. In a marinade for pork or duck. Stirred into oatmeal on a cold morning. In a hot toddy, where it does the job of both the sugar and the bitters.

Buckwheat is also one of the most antioxidant-dense honeys there is, which is nice, but not the reason to eat it. The reason is that spoonful that tastes like a barn in October.
